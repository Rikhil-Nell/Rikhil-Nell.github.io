# Recon 2026: Running a Flagship Fest Between Lab Exams and YC Startup School

Recon is VIT-AP's flagship tech fest. On my resume it says I "led end-to-end planning and execution of a multi-day event." That line is accurate and useless.

Here's the operational version.

## What the role actually became

I served as **Event Director** for Recon 2026, not just scheduling talks, but sponsorship development, speaker outreach, cross-team coordination, website and infrastructure, and keeping an 80-person volunteer org from stalling when momentum died.

Hard numbers:

- **600+ participants**
- **80-person team**
- **₹21 lakh sponsorship** closed in under a month
- First **private VLAN on campus** negotiated with IT for King of the Hill infrastructure
- Event website built and deployed by me

Volunteer organizations drift. Tasks stay unfinished. Sponsors ghost. I spent a large fraction of my time converting intentions into actions, follow-ups, decisions, stakeholder communication, work that disappears once the event succeeds.

## The week that should not have worked

**17 May**: two back-to-back ECE lab exams. No buffer day.

**18 May**: flew to Bangalore for **Y Combinator Startup School**. Selected participant. Full day in rooms with founders from the global cohort, product ideas, validation frameworks, founder-market fit, the kind of conversations you do not get inside a university timetable. That evening: an afterparty where I talked to VCs over drinks. Not networking theater, actual product and market back-and-forth with people who fund companies.

**19 May**: flew back to campus and **inaugurated Recon 2026**.

Three days. Exams, Startup School, founder rooms, VC conversations, cross-country flights, and opening a flagship fest. That week is why I do not treat "student event" as a soft bullet.

## YC Startup School in context

Startup School was not a trophy. It was operating in founder ecosystems while still enrolled, learning validation frameworks, hearing how other builders think about products, and sitting in environments where the default assumption is "build a company," not "finish assignments."

The afterparty mattered too. Informal conversations with VCs are a different channel than conference talks. You learn what investors actually pattern-match on when the slide deck is not in front of them.

Recon and Startup School reinforced the same lesson from different angles: **organizations fail from execution and coordination more often than from lack of intelligence.**

## What I would tell someone doing this next

1. **Write the real facts.** "Closed ₹21 lakh in under a month" does not need the word impressive. It is just a fact.
2. **Infrastructure is leadership.** Getting the VLAN was not IT trivia. It was the difference between a credible CTF infra track and a slide deck.
3. **Invisible work compounds.** Momentum maintenance does not show up on posters. It shows up in whether the event happens at all.

Recon would have happened without me. The question is what version, slower sponsorship, weaker external credibility, less ambitious infra. I helped shape that version.

If you're a student running something larger than your title suggests, document the operational facts while they're still fresh. Future you will undersell otherwise.
